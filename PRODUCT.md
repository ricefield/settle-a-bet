# Settle a Bet — Product Design Context

## Product register

Settle a Bet is a friendly, account-free predictions product for resolving small disagreements. Its visual system borrows the clarity, density, and confidence of modern prediction markets without implying that anything is traded or that real money changes hands.

## Users and their jobs

- Friends want to set up a bet quickly, share simple links, and make their best case.
- Readers want to see what the bet was, who won, and why without decoding formal terminology.
- Skeptical or technical readers want to inspect the sources and model details without making everyone else read them first.

## Purpose

Turn a small disagreement into an entertaining, evidence-backed result while keeping the process fully inspectable.

## Personality

- Sharp: the result is immediate and plainly stated.
- Fair-minded: every Position and dissent gets respectful treatment.
- Social: it should feel at home in a group chat, not a courtroom.
- Transparent: supporting records are complete and easy to find, but do not overpower the story.

## Avoid

- Casino, sportsbook, or gambling visual language.
- A dry audit dashboard or academic report as the primary experience.
- Courtroom language such as Judgment, Verdict, ruling, Judicial Opinion, or Decision Frame in user-facing copy.
- Internal identifiers such as `position-a` in reader-facing copy.
- Hiding uncertainty, dissent, costs, or the fact that the Stake is hypothetical.

## Design principles

1. **Result first.** The winner, winning answer, vote split, and summary belong above the fold.
2. **Explain before documenting.** Show the main reasons next; place full research and technical details later.
3. **Keep the disagreement lively.** Put the competing answers next to each other and let people speak in their own words.
4. **Progressive disclosure builds trust.** Keep the full record available in expandable sections instead of forcing a long wall of data on every reader.
5. **Translate machinery into human language.** Use names, answers, “AI panel,” and “result” in the interface while retaining exact technical identifiers in the details.
6. **Accessibility is part of credibility.** Meet WCAG AA contrast, preserve semantic heading order, support keyboard navigation, and respect reduced-motion preferences.
